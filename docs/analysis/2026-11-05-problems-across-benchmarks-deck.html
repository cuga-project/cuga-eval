Problem 6 of 7

Control can break correct work.

Real run: a rule shown without its exception
Airline rule task, mid model, with the step tracker

The note said: Check that flights are not basic economy (cannot be modified)

The same policy also says: all reservations, including basic economy, can change cabin without changing the flights.

The agent refused. Without the note, it changed the cabin, the passenger and the bags, and passed.

Result over 20 tasks: 17 → 11 passed. 0 better, 6 worse.

Problem 6 of 7

A strong agent gains less, and small-model checks can hurt it.

How others solve it

A log-only or dry-run mode tests rules before they block.AWS AgentCore, Google Semantic Governance. Optional.
It drops checks that fire on over 10% of development calls. Gain did not follow strength: weakest +6.1, Opus 4.6 +41.2, strongest +7.9.A paper from our group.
A second model blocks about half as often as checks on arguments alone.PolicyGuard, authors’ claim.

What we tried on CUGA

+8.8Small agent, same 31 new tasks. Could be chance.
−5.8Strong agent, same tasks, small-model checks. Could be chance.
With a strong checker, the strong agent gained on handbook tasks (problem 2).
Rules from the harms: show exceptions, block once with a quote from the case, report only missing work.
Level switch not built

What we think now

Match the checker to the agent, and never give a strong agent weak checks.
Four control levels: observe, advise, check, control. Hard rules stop work at every level except observe.
Money, external email and delete get strict checks with any model.
The levels are a hypothesis until measured.
