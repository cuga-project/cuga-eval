1 · Why it is hard

Policies come in four shapes, and each needs a different method.

Long document with procedures

20 to 124 pages for one task
Example: offboarding needs written authorization.

Rules scattered in business data

A policy sheet, or an email that changes a rule
Example: an email from the COO replaces an older rule.

Step-by-step flow

A troubleshooting manual with paths and links
Example: one fault leads to another manual section.

Rule list with exceptions

Short rules; the exceptions decide many cases
Example: basic economy cannot be modified, unless the cabin changes first.

4 · The design

One real case through the three checks.

  • Cheapest check first: code, then the yes/no model, then the strong checker.
  • A block needs a quote from the case data, not from the SOP.
  • Every decision is logged with its rule, source and version.

4 · The design

Required work finds what never happened.

Why
Checks at an action cannot see work that was never done.
Expected
At most about +4 to +6 points from names alone. More only where values are known.
Bar before going live
On saved runs first: false alarms at most 10%, catches at least 30%.
Designed, not built yet.
4 · The design

The stronger the agent, the less control we switch on.

  • Hard rules stop work at levels 1 to 3.
  • Money, external email and delete get level 3 checks with any model.
The defaults are a hypothesis until measured. The level switch is not built yet.
